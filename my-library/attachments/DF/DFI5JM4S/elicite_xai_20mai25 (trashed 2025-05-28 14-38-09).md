Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) has gained significant attention due to the widespread use of complex machine learning models that lack interpretability (Vilone & Longo, 2020). The field addresses challenges in explaining AI decisions across various domains, including software engineering (Haji Mohammadkhani et al., 2023). While some algorithms like decision trees offer built-in interpretations, there is no universal approach for all problems (Krishna P. Kalyanathaya & K. K., 2022). XAI methods have been applied mainly to classic ML models rather than more complex ones, with software maintenance and defect prediction being the most studied areas in software engineering (Haji Mohammadkhani et al., 2023). The lack of standard evaluation metrics for XAI methods has led to confusion and a shortage of benchmarks for comparisons (Haji Mohammadkhani et al., 2023). To advance the field, researchers are encouraged to provide more detailed empirical and experimental methods in their reports (Mueller et al., 2019).
